Quantum Field

Quantum Field

Every glowing cloud is one particle that hasn't decided where it is yet. Brighter means "more likely to be found here". Nothing is faked: the clouds are a real solution of the Schrödinger equation, running live.

Play

  • Drag to throw a wave. The longer the drag, the faster it goes (and the tighter its ripples).
  • Waves of the same color are the same particle. Where they overlap you get stripes: in the dark stripes the particle's possibilities cancel each other out. Different colors are different particles — they pass through each other without stripes.
  • Click to look. The number by your cursor is the chance you'll find it there. Either it's there — pop, the whole cloud snaps into a single dot, even the parts far away — or it isn't, and a hole appears where you looked.
  • Hold the mouse down to keep looking. A particle you keep watching can't wander off (the quantum Zeno effect). Move slowly while holding to drag it around with your gaze.
  • Observe all (Space) looks everywhere at once.
  • Double slit: particles go one at a time and each lands as one dot. But the dots build up stripes — each particle went through both slits. Now press Peek at slits: knowing which slit it used makes the stripes vanish.
  • Mic: noise shakes the clouds (the environment disturbing them). A loud clap collapses everything.

Under the hood

  • Solves iħ ∂ψ/∂t = −(ħ²/2m)∇²ψ + V(x,y)ψ on a 256×128 grid (units ħ = m = 1, Δx = 1) using the split-operator Fourier method (Strang splitting): ψ ← e−iVΔt/2 F−1 e−ik²Δt/2 F e−iVΔt/2 ψ. Exactly unitary, 2nd-order accurate. The FFT is written from scratch in the source.
  • Holding the mouse is continuous watching. The first instant is a real "is it in here?" measurement. After that, looking infinitely often confines the evolution to the watched region: the Hamiltonian becomes PHP + QHQ, which is the same as an invisible wall at the edge of your gaze (quantum Zeno dynamics). Drag slowly and the wall herds the particle along.
  • Brightness is the Born-rule probability |ψ|². The moving ripples inside a cloud are its phase arg ψ (toggle Phase to color by it).
  • Clicking is a real measurement (a POVM). A detector with sensitivity W(r) fires with probability Σ W|ψ|². If it fires, the position is pinned down with a Gaussian measurement of width σ: ψ ← e−|r−r₀|²/4σ² ψ, renormalized. If it doesn't, ψ ← √(1−W) ψ — an interaction-free measurement that changes the state without touching the particle.
  • Throwing a wave onto an existing cloud adds it to the superposition: ψ ← ψ/‖ψ‖ + φ, renormalized. Different colors are distinguishable particles with separate wavefunctions, so their densities just add.
  • The screen edges absorb (a smooth damping layer, equivalent to a complex absorbing potential). The double-slit screen records the time-integrated probability flux into the right edge; each run samples one hit from it. Peeking at the slits is a projective measurement of "upper or lower half" just after the slits.
  • Harmonic trap: V = ½ω²r²; a packet with σ = 1/√ω is a coherent state and swings without spreading. Tunneling: a barrier taller than the packet's energy, thin enough to leak through.
  • Mic noise adds a random, moving potential: a crude model of decoherence (dephasing by the environment).
